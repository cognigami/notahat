I’m translating The Little Prince. I’m not a translator. I thought it would be fun.

I’ve been holding on to a copy of Katherine Woods’ translation since I was a little boy. It was published in 1943, alongside the publication of the original, French-language book. I struggled through it as a kid, not because the language was hard but because I wasn’t a natural reader. And English was my second language. Also, it was a bore.

Sure, it’s sold as a classic children’s story. Have you asked who made that decision? Right! the adults. A witty, French meditation on the self-inflicted moral complexity of adult life falls short of interesting reading for a kid.

I ended up studying literature at the graduate level. Before that, I had a short romance with the sciences–we all have some childhood trauma to work out–but I changed my mind, and decided that I wanted to think beautiful thoughts and, occasionally, write them down. 

When I was older, The Little Prince came back into my life. This time I had literary training under my feet, moral complexity in my train, and countless hours spent reading stories to my kids.

This time I noticed the staggering beauty of the language of the story, expressed in French. “On ne voit bien qu’avec le cœur. L’essentiel est invisible pour les yeux.” That line still brings me to tears. Woods’ translation falls a little flat, in contrast, for me: “It is only with the heart that one can see rightly; what is essential is invisible to the eye.”

 * The meter (pattern of stresses in spoken language) is a jumbled mess. 
 * The diction (word choice) forces awkward phrasing: 
    * “It is…” (what is?)
    * “…what is” (what what? and why?)
 * The use of the semi-colon blunts the message.
 * And so on.

There are about 15 translations into English, by all kinds of people. You can see their professional fingerprints in their work. Some are novelists, some are linguists, some are even poets. I think that’s fine, but they’re not my taste.

My goal is to capture the spirit of what Antoine de Saint-Exúpery was trying to make the reader feel, and render it in the most beautiful, poetic English possible, as far as I’m able. 

I’m trying to remain faithful to the emotion, not the words. I’ll document my choices here; chuff or chastise as you see fit.
